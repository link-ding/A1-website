How is the ATAR calculated in NSW?

A plain-English guide to HSC marks, scaling, the best 10 units and how a student’s aggregate becomes an ATAR rank.

Our approach

ATAR is a rank, not an average mark

The Australian Tertiary Admission Rank describes a student’s position relative to their age cohort. It is not the average of their HSC marks, and a mark of 90 does not automatically produce an ATAR of 90.

In NSW, UAC uses scaled marks from eligible courses to form an aggregate. The rules include at least two units of English and the student’s best remaining eligible units, for a total of ten units. That aggregate is then used to determine the ATAR.

3. Best 10 units

The aggregate includes two units of English plus the best eight remaining eligible units.

4. Rank

The aggregate is converted into a percentile rank reported from below 30 up to 99.95.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Does English always count towards the ATAR?

At least two units of English must be included in the ATAR calculation in NSW, even if they are not the student’s highest scaled units.

Is the ATAR an average of HSC marks?

No. UAC uses scaled marks to form an aggregate, then converts that aggregate into a rank.

Can I calculate an exact future ATAR?

No. Scaling and the relationship between aggregates and ATARs vary by cohort and year. Calculators are estimates for planning, not official predictions.
