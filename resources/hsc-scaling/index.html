HSC scaling explained without the myths.

Scaling compares achievement across courses. It does not permanently label a subject “good” or “bad”, and it should not be the only reason to choose one.

Our approach

What scaling is trying to solve

Students take different combinations of HSC courses, so raw marks cannot simply be added as if every course had the same candidature and pattern of achievement. UAC’s scaling process creates a basis for comparison when forming ATAR aggregates.

Scaling reflects how the course candidature performs across their other courses. It is recalculated for each cohort, which is why simplified scaling tables and subject rumours can be misleading. Strong performance remains more useful than choosing a supposedly high-scaling course that does not suit the student.

Common questions

Before you enquire.

Does a difficult subject always scale up?

Not automatically. Scaling is based on candidature performance patterns, not a fixed judgement about course difficulty.

Should I drop a subject because it scales poorly?

Scaling alone is not enough reason. Consider performance, interest, university prerequisites, workload and advice from the school.

Are online scaling calculators exact?

No. Future scaling cannot be known exactly, and public calculators generally use historical or modelled relationships.
